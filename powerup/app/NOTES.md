# PowerUP Malibu Replay, final build

Single-file app: `index.html` (about 433 KB, everything inline, no runtime fetch except the Google Fonts stylesheet). Built from `src/page.html` (markup, CSS, page script), `src/engine.js` (pure simulation, also loaded by node for tests) and `../data.json` by `python3 -I build.py`; never edit `index.html` by hand. Base: build C, with the ladder strip, the real-event line and the MW load chart grafted from build A, the "How PowerUP decides" panel from build B, the rule changes from the lead's review (GRAFT_BRIEF.md items 1 to 9), the round-1 fixes and the round-2 fixes listed at the end of this file.

## What a judge sees, in order

1. **Header**: name, one line on what this is, the simulated Pacific time, the rung chip, a theme button (auto, dark, light; on phone an icon with an aria-label, so the header stays one row at every rung).
2. **PowerUP's ladder** (full width): six step cards, Ordinary day, Watch, Ask, Fill, Hold, Hand to a person (marked forecast), each with its real date and time from the data pack, the current step lit, and one line under the grid saying what PowerUP does at the current step. On a desktop at least 900 px wide and 760 px tall every card carries its own line instead (and every card's title repeats it on hover, so 1280x720 still has the text); the ladder's explanatory paragraph is hidden at 820 px tall or less so the timeline stays above the fold. Each card is a button that jumps the replay to the first full hour after its event (the card's title says where it lands; `#ordinary`, `#watch`, `#ask`, `#fill`, `#hold`, `#hand` also work as hashes). Phone: two rows of 44 px chips with short dates.
3. **Map** (Canvas at devicePixelRatio): ocean and hand-traced coast, SCE lines coloured per circuit (muted until named, bright after, dashed while off, Nicholas grey; drawn under the homes, 2.3 px, and the selected circuit's line drawn again above the plain homes with a thin halo so it is never buried; Serra is dark green, no circuit shares the islanded teal), Tuna and Seaboard dashed approximate bands, 6,874 homes (faint squares at 60%; battery homes as dots coloured by state; Medical Baseline homes as larger diamonds with a dark outline and a cross; silent homes as hollow rings, grey for tower-down and orange for check-this-one; faults as an X), six cell-tower zones with a label carrying a status dot (amber: up, red: down) and a short form that fits the zone's width ("Zone 2: tower down, silent", then "Zone 2: down, silent", then "Zone 2" on two lines, then the number); zone boundaries come from the engine's own zone edges and labels never run under the map buttons, the Bryson Fire from the time it was reported (Oct 15 16:41) with a pulsing ring (static under reduced motion) and a label with the acreage, containment and the time of the latest report, place labels placed with collision avoidance (every label registers its box and later labels move right, left or below; Tuna's band label sits over the water off the band's seaward edge), scale bar, north note. The legend hides itself when the map is under 400 px tall (laptop heights) or on phone, and its button brings it back; on phone it is a compact scrollable overlay with 40 px circuit buttons in four columns. Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, +/-/reset, double-click to zoom. Hover shows a home card; tap or click pins it and selects its circuit (Escape or a tap on empty map closes it; on phone the legend and a pinned card never show together). The hit test is battery-first: a tap inside a Medical Baseline diamond wins, then the nearest battery dot within about 2.4 radii, then a plain home (its card says no battery and names its circuit), then the nearest line, which selects the circuit; on phone a near miss on a line never scrolls the page. A legend entry selects the circuit too.
4. **Timeline**: Oct 7 to Oct 18 18:00 (282 hours) with rung bands, event ticks (hover lists every event within 7 px in time order; ticks closer than 8 px drop to a second row; planned ones dashed; a second dashed tick marks this run's first restoration when the planned length moves it), Today marker, planned shading from the Hand rung (Oct 17 20:00, the forecast end of the Red Flag Warning), fleet soc sparkline, accent cursor. Day gridlines are drawn first and the rung labels sit on a halo over them (HAND included; a label is dropped only when its band is under 30 px); ticks closer than 8 px are clustered with a count badge; a touch tap does not leave a focus ring on the timeline. Play/pause, 1 h, 6 h or 1 day per second, rung jump buttons (desktop), Today, keyboard (arrows, shift+arrows 6 h, PageUp/Down a day, Home/End, space to play). Two lines under it: **PowerUP this hour** (the engine's narrative for the hour) and **Real event** (the latest dated event from the pack at or before the hour, with its source tag and how long ago; an event dated after Today, Sat Oct 17, carries a forecast pill, as it does in the timeline tooltip; when the planned outage length moves the restoration, the line adds this run's first restoration). Desktop: the map height is measured against the header, ladder and timeline so the timeline is above the fold on the first frame at 1280x720 and up. Phone: the rung jumps live in the ladder, both bulletin lines clamp to one line until tapped, the expanded timeline is capped at 30vh of bulletin.
5. **Fleet panel**: subtitle with the battery count, the home count and "(Tuna's 500 assumed)"; counts (with power, Medical Baseline (demo) with power, empty, silent: tower down, silent: check this battery with the count of those really faulted, held at 100% with the filling count, islanded, and after restoration "Grid back: N recharging, M recharge pending"), stacked status bar, reporting channel bar, fleet soc chart with the Medical Baseline power share dashed and the planned region shaded from the Hand rung, outage hours protected, Medical Baseline hours, budget mode counts labelled "homes still off" while any circuit is off (homes back on the grid are counted separately, not as normal), EV dispatch counters, ordinary-day savings and cost of readiness ("as Base and Tesla already do"). Two columns on phone, one-column lists below 1500 px.
6. **Circuit panel**: facts with source or "assumed", past shutoff bars by date, the **October load chart in kW and MW**: SCE's low-to-high October band converted at 27.7 kW per unit, this day's fleet charging stacked on the high (when the day's charging peaks under 5% of the cap because the fleet was already full, the previous day's charging is shown and the caption says so), the cap line labelled in MW, the on-peak window shaded, grid load after restoration, the current hour; this hour's load vs cap in MW with the unit inference stated; battery homes by tier and state in a grid whose rows add up (silent, else empty or faulted or at 0 kWh, else with charge); Serra and Tuna notes.
7. **Approvals panel** (above the Circuit panel, so the officer's decisions sit next to the fleet; the panel head says the officer decides and PowerUP proposes, and the Hold narrative cues the judge to Step 5): (a) restoration recharge plans from the Hand rung, per circuit: order, planned start, and the **peak check reframed as a comparison**: peak at pickup without PowerUP (every home at 1.5x for 2 h, assumed) vs with PowerUP (battery homes ride through on their batteries) vs the cap, as two bars with the cap dashed, the verdict pill with the percent of cap and the kW figures (so rounding to 0.1 MW cannot contradict it), the MW reduction, and when the recharge can start; Approve / Hold per circuit, approve or hold all; the chosen button fills and disables. (b) EV dispatch proposals during the outage (Approve / Hold each, approve all).
8. **How PowerUP decides**: five rules with this hour's numbers for the selected circuit (and the pinned home, if any): headroom cap (with the unit inference and the cap reading), ordinary day, outage budget (instantiated with the inputs the engine used, the charge entering the shutoff hour and the planned length, so the pick equals the engine's mode, then this hour's check and the current mode, with "stepped down from" when it has), silent classification, restoration peak check (pickup factor and stagger marked assumed). Open on desktop, collapsed on phone.
9. **Assumptions drawer**: every number in `assumptions` plus the planned outage length (first, highlighted, with the note "Serra's longest shutoff on record was 163 h; try 96 h to see PowerUP ask for EV trips" and what happens when it is shorter than the real outage), mesh toggle (on by default, a 40 px switch on phone), EV share, donor threshold (55%), kWh per trip, consent share, raised floor (never below the reserve floor: the engine takes the larger of the two), Tier 2 share, dropout share, the three budget modes kept in order (critical <= essential <= normal, a slider that would cross its neighbour is clamped and the drawer says so), the load cap (70 to 100%, with the cap reading spelled out), and a fixed-assumptions line (cold load 1.5x for 2 h, 1 h stagger, 6 km donor radius, 78°F thermostat hold, 12 h consent window, tower jitter, 24 h re-plan, on-peak hours). Reset restores defaults and clears decisions. Any change re-runs the simulation and a line at the top of the drawer says what changed at the current hour; when nothing did, it compares a fingerprint of the whole 282 h run and says either where in the run the change shows or that nothing changed anywhere with the current settings (the EV controls add where to look: a planned outage longer than about 74 h).
10. **What exists, what PowerUP adds** (seven rows from PRIOR_ART.md) and **Sources and data notes** with the caveats.

Fonts: Barlow (UI), Barlow Condensed (labels), IBM Plex Mono (numbers, clock, bulletin) from Google Fonts, with Helvetica Neue/Arial, Arial Narrow and Menlo/Consolas fallbacks. Both themes through tokens; the canvases re-read tokens on `prefers-color-scheme` change and on `data-theme`.

## How the simulation works (src/engine.js, pure functions)

- **Homes**: mulberry32 seeded 20261015. For each named circuit, `customers` homes sampled along SCE polylines by segment length, jittered 40 m; Tuna inside its approximate band. Six zones are equal-count bands by longitude; five have a Starlink node (seeded). A share `adoption_pct` has a battery; tiers by `medical_baseline_pct` and 10% Tier 2; a size factor 0.6 to 1.6 (lognormal-ish, seeded) scales the home's daily use, shaped by the circuit's October max profile (Serra uses Merlin's scaled by customers). Seeded per home: EV ownership, consent delay (90% within 12 h), fault flag and hour, isolated comms dropout window.
- **Window**: 282 hourly steps, Oct 7 00:00 to Oct 18 17:00 (the replay ends Oct 18 18:00). Rung hours are the first hour at or after each timeline event: watch 51 (Oct 9 03:00), ask 129, fill 175, hold 208, hand 260. The ladder cards show the real event minute and their titles say which hour the replay lands on.
- **Ordinary day**: charge off-peak at 10 kW inside the headroom; discharge on-peak to cover load down to the 20% floor; savings = on-peak kWh served x (0.60 - 0.30). **Watch**: Tier 1 fills off-peak and holds. **Ask**: Tier 2 on named circuits fills and holds, Tier 3 floor raised to 50% (the larger of the raised floor and the reserve floor, so a raised floor set under 20% in the drawer never lowers the reserve), consent asked. **Fill**: consenting Tier 3 holds at 100%. All charging goes through a per-circuit, per-hour headroom budget = (cap - October high for that hour) x 27.7 kW, allocated Tier 1 first, on every day. Held homes forfeit on-peak savings, counted as cost of readiness.
- **Hold**: circuits off, batteries islanded with no export. Each home's **outage budget** is the richest of critical 8, essential 16, normal 32 kWh/day (for an average home) **times the home's size factor**, that its charge sustains to the planned restoration; **critical is the floor for everyone, Tier 1 included, and the medical device is inside the critical budget**. The mode is set when the circuit goes off and steps down later only if it stops being sustainable. Hourly load = budget x the circuit's October shape; thermostat hold and EV-charger-paused flags are set. At 0 kWh the home is empty. **Re-planning**: if the planned length passes with a circuit still off and no plan yet (before the Hand rung), PowerUP extends the estimate by 24 h (assumed), so budgets step down and rule 7 keeps a real horizon; the horizon used at every hour is stored per circuit (`horizonH`, `replans`) and the home card reads it ("estimate passed, re-planned once"). At 40 h planned the estimate passes at Oct 17 08:00: 6 medical homes still run dry, every one of them flagged first (no donor is above 55% by then); at 12 h, 40 run dry, all flagged.
- **Comms**: home internet while the grid is on; SMS until the zone's tower battery runs out (`cell_tower_backup_h` plus 0 to 2 h of seeded jitter per zone); then mesh to Starlink where a node exists, else silent (tower-down class, the whole zone in the same hour). Isolated dropouts and faults are the check-this-one class; faulted batteries go dark. The fleet summary counts `faultsActual` only among the silent check-this-one homes (the KPI's "N faulted") and `faults` overall (the status row). Silent homes keep executing their plan. Towers come back when the zone's main circuit is restored.
- **Hand**: at hour 260 PowerUP ranks circuits by Medical Baseline homes dark or under 20%, then Medical Baseline count, then customers. First restoration = max(winds ease + `restore_patrol_h`, shutoff + `planned_outage_h`), which is Oct 18 06:00 at the defaults (both give 62 h); circuits follow **1 h apart** (assumed). Peak check per circuit: pickup = 1.5 x the October high over the 2 pickup hours (assumed); **without PowerUP** every home picks up; **with PowerUP** the share of battery homes expected to still hold charge (faulted batteries excluded) rides through on battery and draws nothing, so the pickup shrinks by that share; both are shown against the cap in MW, with the reduction. The recharge starts at the first hour after the pickup window at which the October high is under the cap, and only when the officer approves; Tier 1 first, inside the remaining headroom. During the 2 h pickup window after restoration a battery with charge rides through islanded and one at 0 kWh reads `standby` (the grid carries the home; it is not "islanded" with nothing to island on). **While the plan is pending or on hold**, a battery on a restored circuit serves its home only down to the reserve floor (the ordinary-day rule), the grid carries the rest, and it draws nothing for recharge: status `standby`, shown as "grid on, recharge pending". The fleet summary's `byMode` counts only homes whose circuit is still off; `gridBack` counts the homes back on the grid, which have no budget. The fleet therefore ends the window at about 16% soc with no "Empty" count, not drained to zero. Circuits come back at the planned hour regardless (SCE's job). All six restorations and recharge starts (Oct 18 06:00 to 13:00) are inside the replay.
- **EV dispatch**: during the outage, a Tier 1 home under 20% whose hours left are shorter than hours to its circuit's horizon gets a proposal. Hours left are **walked forward along the home's hourly shape** (`hoursLeftWalk`, low overnight), not a flat daily rate, so a budget sized to the horizon does not trip at night; the same walk feeds the home card. Donor: nearest reporting Tier 3 EV home within `ev_radius_km` (6 km, assumed) with battery above `ev_donor_min_pct` (default 55%). An approved trip takes the donor out of the pool for 24 h; a pending or held one only for 3 h. Approval adds 10 kWh one hour later. Unmet needs (no donor, or none within the radius) are recorded and shown.
- **Narrative** (`narrate`): one line per hour. The Fill line reports the selected circuit's charging against the headroom left after that charging (post-allocation), and when nothing charges because the fleet was already full it says so and gives the unused headroom. The Hand line sums charging and headroom over every approved circuit past its pickup window ("667 batteries recharging, 2.0 MW inside 2.0 MW headroom on Galahad, Cuthbert"), notes batteries riding through the pickup window, batteries holding at the floor on other circuits and circuits still off; it never names a circuit that is not charging and never double counts. Counts are pluralised and the restoration reason lists dark and under-20% separately.
- Per-hour fleet summaries and per-circuit load arrays are precomputed once per run so scrubbing is instant. Decisions (restore per circuit, EV per proposal id) are inputs to the run.

### Why the planned outage length matters (lead's item 5)

At the defaults (62 h planned, restoration at 62 to 67 h) every Medical Baseline home lasts: a full 39.2 kWh battery carries even a 1.6x home's critical budget (12.8 kWh/day) for 73 h, and smaller homes get essential. No EV trip is needed, and the Approvals panel says so.

With a flat 8 kWh/day budget a full battery lasts 117 h, so "under 20%" could never happen inside a replay that ends 74 h into the outage, whatever the planned length. Two modelling choices, both labelled as assumptions on the page, make the lead's "try 96 h" story work honestly:

1. The budget follows the home's own hourly shape scaled to the mode (the spec's wording), so a large home in critical mode uses up to 12.8 kWh/day and a small one as little as 4.8.
2. The planned outage length also holds the simulated restoration (SCE's own estimate): first restoration = max(hand + patrol, hold + planned). Raising the patrol length has the same effect.

At 96 h the largest Medical Baseline homes (size over about 1.27) cross 20% from Oct 18 03:00 with 30 or more hours still to go, and small Tier 3 EV homes still hold over 55%: at 15% adoption that is 4 proposals, all with a donor within 6 km, 0 unmet. More adoption or a larger Medical Baseline share gives more.

## Assumptions and honesty

Every assumed number carries "assumed" where shown (the Approvals pickup line, math rows 3 and 5, the unmet-donor reason, the drawer); restoration is labelled planned and everything from the Hand rung on is shaded as forecast or planned; Medical Baseline homes are demo homes, and the fleet KPI and tier grid say "(demo)" and "(assumed)" where the counts are read; SCE load units are an inference (amps per phase at 16 kV, 1 A = 27.7 kW), stated on the chart, in the circuit panel and in math rows 1 and 5. Assumptions introduced beyond the data pack, each a drawer control or on the drawer's fixed-assumptions line: Tier 2 share 10%, consent 90% within 12 h, raised floor 50%, 10 kW grid charge cap, 2% isolated comms dropouts, 0 to 2 h tower jitter, 6 km donor radius, cold load 1.5x for 2 h, 1 h stagger, 78°F thermostat hold, 24 h re-plan, 25% EV share, 10 kWh per trip, donor threshold 55%, budgets scaled by home size, the planned outage length holding the restoration. **Cap reading**: the engine treats `cold_load_cap_pct` as a ceiling on total circuit load (cap = October peak x share; headroom = cap minus the hour's October high), which differs from the spec's "(peak minus high) x share" whenever the share is not 100%; the drawer note and math row 1 say so, and the slider now tops out at 100% (where both readings agree) so the fleet can never push a circuit above its October peak. The fleet subtitle says Tuna's 500 homes are assumed, the budget sub-line and the Ask card say 50% is assumed, the thermostat hold says 78°F (assumed), the legend says Medical Baseline (demo). The prior-art table credits Tesla Storm Watch, Base ("Base says", as the prior-art check words it), SCE, SPAN, IEEE 1547, CSIP 4.4.2, Meshtastic and Starlink; the ordinary-day panel and the Step 0 card say "as Base and Tesla already do".

## Known gaps and things the lead should know

1. EV trips at 96 h are few at 15% adoption (4 of 50 medical homes) because only the largest homes cross 20% inside the window; the count is what the rules produce. The note in the Approvals panel and the drawer tells a judge where to look.
2. At 15% adoption the pickup stays over the cap on five of six circuits even with PowerUP (the batteries take 0.5 to 1.2 MW off it); the recharge then waits until the circuit is under the cap, and the panel says to raise adoption to see every circuit pass. The cold-load pickup of homes without a battery is a 1.5x multiplier on SCE's October high, not a model of appliance behaviour.
3. With a planned outage longer than about 74 h the restorations fall outside the replay window; the plans still appear at the Hand rung with their planned times, the real-event line and a second dashed tick say where this run's first restoration falls, and the circuits stay off to the end of the window. With a planned outage shorter than 62 h PowerUP re-plans in 24 h steps when the estimate passes; the budgets were sized to the shorter estimate, so some medical homes still run dry before Oct 18 06:00 and by then no Tier 3 home is above the 55% donor threshold: PowerUP flags them ("no donor") rather than staying silent, which is what the rules produce when SCE's estimate is wrong.
4. Nearly every battery is full at the 16:00 shutoff because off-peak charging fills them each night (right after on-peak, 21:00 to 23:00), so the Fill rung changes status (held vs watch) and readiness cost more than state of charge; the load chart then shows the previous night's charging, labelled as such. The reserve floor, battery output, normal budget and EV sliders change little at the Hold hour; the drawer's delta line says when a re-run changed nothing at the current hour.
5. Restoration order can change when EV approvals change which medical homes are at risk; decisions are keyed by circuit so they carry over.
6. Fonts load only when online; the fallbacks are Helvetica Neue/Arial, Arial Narrow and Menlo/Consolas. The screenshots in `shots/` were taken offline and show the fallbacks.
7. The on-peak hours themselves are not adjustable (rates are).
8. After restoration the batteries hold at the floor until the officer approves the recharge, so the end-of-window fleet soc is about 16% (many batteries were already under 20% after 62 h on a critical budget); a judge who wants to see the recharge should approve the plans at the Hand rung.
9. On phone the five rung jump buttons are hidden from the sticky timeline (the ladder cards are the jump UI) to keep the timeline under a quarter of the screen; hashes and the ladder still jump to every rung.
10. The desktop legend covers the south-west corner of the map (Galahad's west segment) when it is open; it now starts hidden whenever the map is under 400 px tall (1280x720, 1366x768) and on phone, and the selected line is drawn above the homes with a halo. On phone the legend is a compact scrollable overlay rather than a bottom sheet.
11. At 1280x720 the ladder cards do not carry their what-lines (there is no room above the fold); the line under the grid and each card's hover title carry the text. From 760 px tall every card has its line.
12. Wording at the Hand rung: the ladder chip says "(planned)", the card says "Winds ease (forecast)", and the real-event line and tooltip put a "forecast" pill on any event dated after Today. The two words are kept on purpose: the winds easing is a forecast; the restoration is a plan.
13. The cap slider stops at 100%, where the engine's cap reading and the spec's headroom formula agree; below 100% they differ as described under Assumptions and honesty, and the drawer says so.
